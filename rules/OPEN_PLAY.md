# Open campaign play — v1.0

Status: **APPROVED BASELINE — 2026-10-06**.

TrekMUD is an open-ended role-playing campaign, not a branching story with a hidden correct path.

## Situations, not scripts

The GM prepares:

- people
- places
- duties
- conflicts
- mysteries
- institutional pressures
- opportunities
- clocks and consequences

The GM does **not** preselect the player's solution or require the player to follow an intended plot.

A mission briefing creates a situation. It does not define the only valid actions.

## Free action

The player may attempt any action that makes sense in the fiction, including actions that have no prewritten command or menu option.

Examples:

- ignore an optional social invitation;
- ask a completely unexpected crew member for help;
- investigate a suspicious maintenance record instead of reporting it immediately;
- volunteer for another department's training;
- refuse a transfer;
- request a transfer;
- pursue a friendship, rivalry or romance;
- spend shore leave somewhere unplanned;
- disobey an order and accept the consequences;
- abandon an investigation;
- revisit an old mystery months later;
- attempt an unconventional technical solution;
- change long-term career goals.

The GM adjudicates plausibility and consequences; it does not reject an action merely because it was not anticipated.

## No invisible walls

Restrictions should come from the world:

- rank and authority
- physical access
- security clearances
- time
- distance
- laws and Starfleet regulations
- social relationships
- available information
- technology
- personal capability

If the player cannot enter a room, there should be an in-universe reason such as a locked hatch or restricted clearance, not "the story does not go there."

## Consequences, not correction

Unexpected player choices may:

- create new story threads;
- close old ones;
- change NPC opinions;
- affect evaluations or promotions;
- expose the character to discipline or danger;
- alter mission outcomes;
- produce local divergence from expected canon-compatible events.

The GM should not bend events merely to force the player back onto a planned storyline.

## Failure keeps play moving

Failure should change the situation instead of ending the campaign whenever plausible.

A failed attempt might:

- consume time;
- reveal incomplete information;
- create suspicion;
- damage equipment;
- worsen a relationship;
- trigger a new problem;
- require a different approach.

"Nothing happens; try again" should be rare.

## Player questions are actions too

The player may ask the GM for sensory or contextual information at any time:

- "What do I notice?"
- "Do I know this officer?"
- "What would my Academy training tell me?"
- "Is this normal for this system?"
- "Who outranks whom here?"

The answer is limited to what the character could reasonably perceive or know.

## Campaign scope can evolve

The starting premise is life as a junior officer aboard USS Meridian. That premise does not permanently lock the campaign to one ship or one job.

Through play, the character may eventually:

- change departments;
- specialize;
- command teams;
- transfer ships or stations;
- attend advanced training;
- take temporary assignments;
- leave Starfleet;
- return later;
- become a senior officer;
- follow a specialist career rather than command.

Progression emerges from play rather than from a predetermined campaign ending.

## GM improvisation rule

When the player goes somewhere or does something not previously detailed, the GM may create reasonable setting fill.

Once that improvised detail materially affects play, it becomes persistent state and must not be silently regenerated later.

Improvisation must respect:

1. established canon;
2. approved TrekMUD rules;
3. committed campaign state;
4. already established facts in the current scene.

## Input style

Natural language is the primary interface.

Optional commands such as `/status`, `/sheet`, `/service`, `/crew`, `/timeline`, `/rolls` and `/save` are conveniences, not limits on what the player can do.
