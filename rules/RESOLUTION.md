# Resolution system — proposed v1.0

Status: **proposal**.

## First question: should there be a roll?

Most professional actions do **not** require a roll.

If a trained Starfleet officer has adequate time, tools and information to perform a routine task, the task succeeds. Dice are used only when the outcome is meaningfully uncertain and failure would create an interesting consequence.

A roll should have declared stakes before it happens.

If an action is physically impossible, contradicts established facts, or requires authority/access the character plainly does not possess, **do not roll to see if reality bends**. Explain the in-universe barrier and let the player decide what to do about it.

## Core check

When uncertainty matters:

`2d6 + Attribute + Skill + one relevant Specialty + situational modifiers`

Only one specialty can contribute to a roll.

Normal situational modifiers are kept between **-3 and +3 total**.

The GM identifies the relevant Attribute, Skill, specialty eligibility and target before the roll whenever doing so would not reveal information the character could not know. A player may propose a different approach; changing the fictional approach can legitimately change the Attribute, Skill, target or whether a roll is needed.

## Difficulty

| Difficulty | Target | Typical meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Pressured routine | 7 | basic task under time pressure or distraction |
| Professional challenge | 9 | meaningful work for a trained officer |
| Difficult | 11 | demanding technical, social or field problem |
| Severe | 13 | unusually hard even for experienced personnel |
| Extraordinary | 15 | exceptional achievement under difficult conditions |

If a task is easier than target 7 for the acting professional, it should normally be automatic instead of rolled.

## Outcome by margin

- **Target +4 or more:** Exceptional success
- **Target to Target +3:** Success
- **Target -1:** Near miss; when plausible the GM may offer success with a meaningful cost/complication, otherwise failure
- **Target -2 to Target -4:** Failure
- **Target -5 or worse:** Severe failure, but only when severe consequences were plausible and established before the roll

A low roll does not invent absurd incompetence. Failure describes the situation: insufficient time, incomplete information, resistance, equipment limits, an unexpected complication, or a genuine mistake appropriate to the circumstances.

### No automatic criticals or fumbles

A natural **12** on 2d6 is not automatically a miraculous success, and a natural **2** is not automatically catastrophic.

The final result is determined by the total, target, margin and fictional circumstances. Very high or very low natural dice may color the description, but they never override what is plausible.

## Repeated attempts

If the situation has not materially changed, the player cannot simply repeat the identical roll until it succeeds.

A new roll requires a meaningful change such as:

- a different approach;
- new information;
- better tools;
- help from another person;
- more time at a real cost;
- changed conditions.

Otherwise the previous result stands and play moves forward.

## Untrained attempts

Skill rank 0 does not automatically prohibit an attempt.

An untrained character may attempt something if it is plausible, using the normal check with Skill 0. Some highly specialized actions require a qualification, specialized equipment, or professional authorization before a roll is possible at all.

## Assistance

A materially useful helper with relevant competence normally grants **+1**.

Exceptionally coordinated team assistance may grant **+2 total**, never more through ordinary stacking.

If several people are each independently doing important work, resolve their tasks separately instead of turning the group into one giant modifier.

## Extended work

Long technical, scientific, medical or investigative problems should not become repeated identical dice rolls.

Instead use:

1. establish the problem;
2. identify meaningful approaches;
3. resolve only uncertain turning points;
4. let successful routine work happen automatically;
5. advance time and consequences honestly.

## Social actions

Social checks resolve uncertainty in how another person responds; they do **not** erase agency.

A successful roll can persuade, reassure, intimidate, deceive, negotiate or improve the odds of cooperation when the request is plausible for that NPC.

A roll cannot force someone to:

- betray a deeply held value without sufficient leverage or context;
- become a friend or romantic partner;
- forget established history;
- reveal information they genuinely do not know;
- obey an impossible or suicidal request merely because the dice were high.

Relationships develop through persistent interaction and circumstances, not one persuasion roll.

## Opposed situations

Prefer a fixed target representing the opposition when only the player's uncertainty matters.

Use opposed rolls only when two active parties are simultaneously contesting an outcome and both results meaningfully matter.

## Hidden rolls and hidden targets

Perception, deception detection and some uncertainty may be rolled privately to avoid revealing information the character does not possess.

A target may also remain hidden when knowing its exact value would itself reveal something. The roll, target and modifiers are still recorded for later audit even if they are not immediately shown.

The underlying random result remains auditable through the deterministic RNG system.
