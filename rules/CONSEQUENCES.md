# Health, fatigue and lasting consequences — v1.0

Status: **APPROVED BASELINE — 2026-10-06**.

TrekMUD does not use hit points.

## Injury state

The character has one overall injury state plus specific conditions.

Overall states:

1. **Healthy**
2. **Minor injury**
3. **Significant injury**
4. **Severe injury**
5. **Critical**
6. **Incapacitated**

Specific conditions describe what actually happened: burn, fracture, concussion, radiation exposure, phaser wound, decompression injury, etc.

Penalties apply only when the injury would reasonably interfere with the attempted action.

Medical treatment can stabilize a condition without instantly erasing it. Recovery consumes believable in-universe time unless established Star Trek medicine can reasonably accelerate it.

## Fatigue

Fatigue is tracked from **0–4**.

- **0 Rested** — no effect
- **1 Tired** — descriptive effect; no automatic modifier
- **2 Fatigued** — normally -1 to sustained checks meaningfully affected by fatigue
- **3 Exhausted** — normally -2 to affected checks; continued duty requires justification
- **4 Spent** — serious impairment; sleep, relief or medical attention becomes necessary

Fatigue is not punishment for role-playing ordinary life. It matters when the character repeatedly gives up rest, endures crisis conditions or works unusually long shifts.

## Permanent consequences

A single unlucky mundane roll should not arbitrarily kill or permanently ruin a long-running character.

Permanent injury, death, court-martial, career-ending discipline or similar outcomes require circumstances where that consequence was genuinely plausible.

Risk may still become severe. TrekMUD protects continuity from arbitrary cruelty, not the character from meaningful choices.

## Professional consequences

Persistent non-physical consequences include:

- commendations
- reprimands
- poor or exceptional evaluations
- loss or gain of command confidence
- qualification restrictions
- reassignment
- missed promotion
- temporary relief from duty
- changes in reputation

Consequences are recorded in the service record instead of being forgotten when a scene ends.
